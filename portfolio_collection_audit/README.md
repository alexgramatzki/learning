# 2026 Portfolio – Collection Completeness Audit

**Source:** Syncopate exports of the *2026 Portfolio* group (51 acquired catalogs, 85 payors, statements 2001 – 2026 Q3, $124.6M net all-time) plus Viberate audience-by-country and radio-spin data for the 16 artist-linked catalogs.
**Deliverable:** `2026_Portfolio_Collection_Audit.xlsx` (22 sheets). This note explains what was tested, what we found, how confident each finding is, and how to use the workbook for payor outreach.

---

## 1. The approach in one paragraph

Every payor is judged against three independent yardsticks, never against theory alone:

1. **The same catalog seen through other payors.** On one catalog the label pays on the recording, the publisher on the composition and the PRO on the writer share of the same songs. If the label shows Deezer streams in Germany and the publisher shows no Deezer and no Germany, the publisher has a gap on songs we know are earning.
2. **The same payor on other catalogs.** If BMI or Kobalt reports Peloton income for one of our writers, it holds the licence for all of them. A payor that reports a DSP on three catalogs and not on a fourth is a matching/registration problem, not a licensing problem.
3. **External consumption.** Viberate Spotify listeners by country and monthly radio spins tell us where the music is consumed, independently of any statement.

A finding only becomes a **FAIL** when at least one strong yardstick (1 or 2) corroborates it. Anything that rests on a single weak signal, or where a benign explanation exists, is a **REVIEW**. Where the payor lumps income into "Other" so the test cannot run, it is **UNVERIFIABLE**, and the first ask is for better data, not an accusation.

## 3. How to use the workbook
1. **Scorecard – Catalogs / Scorecard – Payors**: FAIL/REVIEW counts per test and an order-of-magnitude $ at risk. Sort by $ to pick the first conversations.
2. **Outreach Questions**: one row per (payor, catalog, issue) with a drafted question and a *verify first* step. Do the verify step, then send.
3. **T1–T11 sheets**: evidence rows behind every status, with the counter-hypotheses in the *note* column.
4. **DSP Matrix** sheets: payor × DSP $ for the last two years, the "we see Universal collecting from X, we don't see you" view.
5. **README sheet**: status definitions and, for each test, why we might be right, why we might be wrong, and how to test.

## 4. Testing our own conclusions before outreach
* **Confirm the window.** PRO and foreign income lags 9–36 months. Every test uses each payor's own last 8 statement quarters; a gap that closes on the next statement was lag.
* **Confirm ownership.** Label income on a song is not proof we own the composition. Check the deal schedule (T6) and performer rights (T11) first.
* **Confirm the data.** Where *Unverifiable $* is above 30% for a payor, request detail rather than asserting a gap.
* **Look for the successor.** A stale payor with a successor is an accounting question (final period, pipeline), not a collection failure.
* **Sample at line level.** For anything that survives, pull two raw statements (one from each side of the comparison) for the same song and quarter and show them side by side in the outreach.

## 5. Limits of this pass
Registrations (MLC) coverage is 50 rows per batch and only certain publishers match by Song ID, so sheet T9 is indicative only. Rate comparisons (T7) cannot separate collection problems from ownership share and admin fees. Viberate data is artist-level and current, so it is a proxy for the catalog's works. Estimated $ at risk is for prioritisation, not for a claim.
